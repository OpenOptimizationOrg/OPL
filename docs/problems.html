Icing is also a risky condition for airplane wings since it may induce flow separation at a small angles of attack, which then might lead to stall and consequently loss of lift. Ice accretion is a complex process which depends on both aerodynamics and thermodynamics. The process is affected by many parameters, for instance, ambient temperature, surface properties, relative speed of the airfoil, and the time span over which the icing event takes place. As a result, numerous possibilities for an ice profile exist. Bragg et al. categorized the leading-edge ice on the airfoils into four types: roughness, horn, streamwise, and spanwise-ridge ice.
